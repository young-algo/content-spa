# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Personal knowledge workers, researchers, and power users who collect articles, YouTube transcripts, PDFs, Markdown, and notes into a local archive. They use the interface during focused review, retrieval, triage, and synthesis sessions where speed, density, and confidence matter more than decoration.

## Product Purpose

Content Index is a local personal archive dashboard for ingesting, browsing, searching, clustering, and asking questions over a private content library. Success means users can quickly add material, understand what is in the archive, find relevant items, and turn retrieved context into useful answers or synthesized notes without leaving the workflow.

## Brand Personality

Dense command center: analytical, compact, and power-user oriented. The interface should feel like a trustworthy instrument panel for a private research corpus, with quiet confidence, high information throughput, and clear task affordances.

## Anti-references

Avoid SaaS marketing clichés: glossy gradients, hero metrics, decorative card grids, glassmorphism, oversized empty-state illustrations, and promotional page tropes. Avoid interfaces that prioritize brand spectacle over retrieval, reading, and archival workflow clarity.

## Design Principles

1. **Retrieval first.** Search, browse, topics, and document actions should stay immediately reachable and unambiguous.
2. **Density with legibility.** Preserve compact information display, but never at the cost of contrast, readable hierarchy, or scannable grouping.
3. **State over decoration.** Use color, motion, and emphasis to communicate selection, progress, freshness, errors, and next actions—not ornament.
4. **Local-tool confidence.** The UI should feel stable, private, and operational, like a reliable desktop-grade archive rather than a hosted growth product.
5. **Progressive detail.** Summaries, tags, scores, and metadata should reveal enough to choose the next action without overwhelming the primary scan path.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No special accessibility baseline was requested. Future UI work should still preserve standard product-quality implementation care: readable contrast, keyboard operability, visible focus states, semantic controls, and reduced-motion alternatives where motion is introduced.
